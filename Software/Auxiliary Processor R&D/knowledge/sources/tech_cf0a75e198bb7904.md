Parking Frequently Asked Questions

Learn more about the university’s plan to improve parking experiences for students,
 faculty and staff. 

As universities across the country grow, campuses are taking a thoughtful approach
 to parking and transportation — balancing convenient campus access with pedestrian
 and bicyclist safety, the preservation of green spaces and responsible investment
 in parking infrastructure, which is typically not eligible for public funding.

At Tennessee Tech, we recognize that parking is an important part of how you experience
 campus. Here are the facts about parking at Tech, along with the university’s immediate
 and long-term plans to provide additional parking options for students, faculty and
 staff.

 Key Facts

Tennessee Tech has reduced parking costs for students: Beginning this semester, Tennessee Tech replaced the $130-per-semester student parking
 permit with a $100-per-semester transportation charge included within each student’s
 fees. Students who park on campus will save $60 during the 2026-27 academic year compared
 with the previous year.

Parking infrastructure is not eligible for public funding: The university cannot access public funding for parking infrastructure like it can
 for new academic buildings. The university must fund new parking infrastructure through
 university resources.

University employees also pay to park: Tennessee Tech employees – from the president to the newest faculty and staff hires
 – also pay for parking. Most university employees pay between $280 to $310 for general
 parking. This academic year, university employees were also given the option to purchase
 a reserved parking spot on a first-come, first-served basis for $800.

 What is Tennessee Tech’s current parking inventory?

Tennessee Tech currently has nearly 5,000 parking spaces across campus. Nearly 4,000
 spaces can be used by students or employees. Approximately 750 spaces are designated
 specifically for employees, while approximately 40 spaces are designated for visitors. 

The current parking inventory represents a modest reduction from last year’s available
 parking due to construction perimeters for the university’s new Advanced Construction
 and Manufacturing Engineering (ACME) Building and J.J. Oakley Innovation Center &
 Residence Hall. 

Still, the university continues to uphold best practices for university parking which
 recommend one parking space for roughly every four students. Importantly, not all
 students, faculty and staff are on campus at any one time. 

 Long-Term Plan: New Parking Garage

In a historic first, Tennessee Tech’s Board of Trustees recently approved construction
 of a new parking garage in the heart of campus with more than 800 parking spaces.
 The future garage will be constructed where Tech’s tennis courts are located today
 – meaning no existing parking spaces will be lost during construction. The tennis
 courts will be relocated across Willow Avenue. The parking garage will soon enter
 the design phase, with construction expected to be completed in fall 2028.

 Short-Term Plan:   Continuous Shuttle Improvements, Expanded Parking Capacity

Tennessee Tech continues to expand parking availability and improve transportation
 options for students, faculty and staff.

The North Whitney Avenue parking lot has reopened, with more than 90% of the lot now
 available for permitted student and employee parking. Access is available via Wings
 Up Way, while the West 10th Street entrance remains temporarily closed due to construction.
 A temporary turnaround is in place at the north end of the lot as work continues on
 the J.J. Oakley Innovation Center & Residence Hall.

The university also continues to make additional spaces available in the rear West
 Lot across Willow Avenue and is fine-tuning operations for its free campus shuttle
 system to improve wait times and efficiency.

 Where are my best options to find parking on weekdays during the semester?

Parking remains available every day in the purple-zoned West Lot across Willow Avenue,
 near Tech Village and the Intramural Fields. 

Regardless of where you park, a free and convenient shuttle is ready to take you from
 your parking spot to destinations across campus from 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. each day that
 classes are in session. No sign-up or pass is required – simply hop on and hop off.

You can visit this webpage or download the PassioGo! app in the Apple Store or on Google Play to see real-time updates on the shuttles' locations. 

 Are there alternative ways to get to campus?

For some students, getting to class on two wheels can be a great way to save costs
 and stay closer to the center of campus. 

Bike racks are available at many buildings across campus, with the largest being a
 covered space behind the Roaden University Center – near the fire pit and the ground
 floor entrance. Construction on a larger indoor, controlled-access bicycle shelter
 is underway now.

For those without a bicycle of their own, Tech's Bike Share program allows students to check out a bicycle, helmet and bike lock for up to two weeks
 at a time. Stop by the Marc L. Burnett Student Recreation & Fitness Center to learn
 more.

 How can students provide feedback?

University administrators have met regularly with a student workgroup and with members
 of the Student Government Association as they weigh parking decisions. Students can
 also provide feedback at parking@tntech.edu or through their elected SGA leadership.
